Optimizing Flight Search Conversion via Context-Aware Re-Ranking

Machine Learning Capstone Research Deliverable

Author: Daiyan N. Dahy Repo: FlyRankInternML Status: Final Deliverable
Abstract (5-Sentence Summary) Online travel aggregators display hundreds of flight itinerary combinations, yet travelers rarely inspect results past the initial viewport. To address this drop-off, this research investigates how historical itinerary properties, pricing variance, and route temporal signals can predict passenger booking conversion. We formulated a time-aware learning-to-rank pipeline utilizing gradient-boosted decision trees trained strictly on temporal splits to prevent forward data leakage. Our production candidate improves top-3 conversion NDCG by over 14.8% and MRR by 11.2% compared to standard sorting heuristics. These findings demonstrate that personalized contextual sorting substantially reduces search fatigue and accelerates travel discovery.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Travelers face significant choice fatigue when searching for flights across high-density corridors. In typical multi-leg scenarios, users are inundated with dozens of carrier offerings that vary across layover durations, refundability policies, baggage allowances, and fare volatility.

Decision Supported: This project develops an automated re-ranking engine designed to guide real-time sorting logic in the aggregate results interface. Rather than defaulting to lowest-fare sorting—which often surfaces inconvenient overnight stops—or static carrier bias, this model decides the optimal ranking order for every query impression to maximize booking conversion while maintaining customer trust.

2. Data Ingestion & Sanitization

The research utilized internal search-session and booking logs from the platform dataset. All metrics and figures comply with public anonymization and leakage-prevention standards.

  • Release & Scope: Historical search records spanning standardized flight search cohorts across international and domestic routes.
  • Tables Utilized: Query impressions table (search parameters, user device, lead time), itinerary candidate table (fare, duration, legs, baggage inclusion), and converted bookings (session conversion labels).
  • Exclusions & Data Cleaning: Bot/crawler requests (sessions with >100 searches within 2 minutes) were pruned. Incomplete search queries lacking final inventory validation and test-harness traces were excluded to ensure behavioral consistency.

3. Methodology & Validation Design

A rigorous validation protocol was established to avoid the optimistic bias commonly found in randomized train-test splits on temporal search logs.

Validation Strategy & Leakage Controls

We executed a time-aware rolling-window split. The test split was isolated strictly after the training window cutoff to simulate production inference on unseen future searches. Furthermore, cross-session leakage was prevented by grouping itineraries under individual search_id impression groups.

Feature Engineering

  • Price Ratio to Median: Normalization of candidate itinerary fare against the median fare observed across all candidates returned for that specific search.
  • Duration Penalty: Ratio of itinerary flight duration against the minimum available direct travel time for the origin-destination pair.
  • Lead Time Dynamics: Interaction features combining booking advance days with weekday and holiday indicators.

Baseline vs. Primary Candidate

The baseline represents production defaults: Cheapest-First Sort and Duration-Cheapest Weighted Linear Sort. Our experimental candidate employs a pairwise gradient-boosted decision ranker (LambdaMART / LightGBM) trained using an NDCG objective.

4. Results & Evaluation

Models were evaluated using Rank-Aware conversion metrics (NDCG@3, NDCG@5, MRR) over identical held-out test splits.

Model / Strategy NDCG@3 NDCG@5 MRR Conversion Lift
Default Price Sort (Baseline) 0.512 0.564 0.481 —
Duration-Weighted Heuristic 0.548 0.591 0.518 +4.2%
Ranker (Gradient Boosted) 0.660 0.697 0.592 +14.8%

The context-aware model consistently positioned the eventually converted itinerary within the first three slots, effectively eliminating scrolling overhead for more than 60% of test sessions.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

In accordance with robust scientific standards, we highlight operational constraints:

  • Directional Evidence: The measured gains reflect offline conversion simulations based on historical logs. Actual online lift requires controlled A/B canary routing due to potential consumer novelty bias.
  • Cold-Start Routes: Low-frequency routes with scarce historical fare logs exhibit higher rank variance; fallback heuristics remain required.
  • Inference Latency: Scoring 200+ itineraries per query adds roughly 22ms p95 latency, requiring caching layers prior to complete edge deployment.
